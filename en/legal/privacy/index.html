Controller and contact

FORMACOM ET ASSOCIES (NELFETECH), 63 rue de la Liberté, 92220 Bagneux, France, determines the purposes and means of the processing described here. Data requests can be sent to contact@nelfeplay.com.

Player accounts

You sign in with a single-use link sent by email: no password is stored. We keep your address, its fingerprint (used for uniqueness without comparing addresses in clear), your nickname, your avatar, a player code, your notification preferences and, if you declare it, your country. The legal basis is the performance of the contract that gives you access to the service (terms of use).

Your nickname is never derived from your address, which often contains a first and last name. The country is optional: it is used for country rankings and for our audience statistics, as totals.

A signed-in session lasts at most one year, extended on each visit. You can delete your account: its data is erased, and its scores remain ranked without a name or nickname (see “Certified scores and replays”).

What is public

Everyone can see: your nickname, your avatar, your country if declared, your ranked scores and times with their date and replay, your Multiplayer and Mass Run ratings, and your player page. Your email address is never published.

The site’s public pages, rankings included, are copied to the backup copies described in the legal notice.

Certified scores and replays

At the end of a game, your machine sends a signed passport: the game, the score or time, the duration, the settings and fingerprints of the emulator, the game and its memory definition, the machine identifier and your account. These are used to certify the score and detect cheating; a doubtful score is set aside for review. The legal basis is the performance of the contract (the ranking is the service you request) and our legitimate interest in reliable rankings.

A game’s replay records the inputs played, with no image of you and no sound from your room. It is published with the score: anyone can watch or replay it. Other machines on the network may keep and serve it, and it is copied as a backup to GitHub and GitLab. Record replays also serve as ghosts in other players’ speedruns.

Record films are published on the Nelfe Play YouTube channel, with the nickname, the game and the score in the title. A film shorter than ten seconds is not published.

Each batch of scores is timestamped by a fingerprint written into the Bitcoin blockchain (OpenTimestamps). This fingerprint contains no personal data and identifies no one; it cannot be erased, which guarantees that a score was not altered afterwards.

When an account is deleted, its scores remain ranked without a name or nickname, as a ranking is a fact; its Mass Run rating is erased. On request, we also make private the YouTube films published under its name.

Multiplayer

To play together, we process your online presence, your rooms, invitations and match proposals, results, your rating, as well as abandons and conduct reports, to keep fair rankings and protect players.

During a match, the connection may be direct between machines: each one then sees the other’s IP address. It may also go through a Nelfe Play relay, which then sees the machines’ addresses.

The player hosting a match may stream it live: the game image and the players’ nicknames are then public.

Mass Run

During a race, your machine sends the race server your position, your character’s image, your nickname and your avatar colour. This information is shared in real time with the other runners and is only used for the race, of which only the ranking and the resulting rating are kept. A race has no replay.

These servers may be NelfeNode nodes run by community members. They do not receive your email address; they see your machine’s IP address.

Live streams, NelfePlay TV and replay rooms

When you start a live stream, your game image and your nickname are broadcast publicly on the site and, where applicable, on YouTube. Viewers’ reactions and presence in a live stream or a replay room are processed to display them.

Community, votes and notifications

You can follow players; two players who follow each other appear as friends. You can stop following at any time.

Your vote for the next game opened to scoring is linked to your account; only each game’s vote count is published.

Notifications are shown on the site. If you allow browser notifications, we keep the subscription address your browser provides; its vendor’s push service (Google, Mozilla, Apple or Microsoft) then carries encrypted messages. You can turn them off at any time from your account or your browser.

Paired machines and installations

A machine you pair is registered with a name YOU choose, a public key and a technical identifier. We keep which games are installed on it (your gamelist), so your account can show it and an installation is not repeated endlessly. You can revoke a machine at any time: its authorisation ends immediately.

Licences and packages issued for a machine contain neither your address nor your name: only an account identifier, the machine identifier and an expiry date.

Measuring what is played

Machines send us the title launched, the system, the play duration and, where applicable, the declared country. They NEVER send who is playing. These measurements show the real audience of titles and support discussions with their rights holders. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in measuring use of the service.

Counting distinct people without naming them requires a specific method: for one month, each account is reduced to a token computed with a secret salt. When the month closes, the totals are kept and THE SALT IS DESTROYED. The tokens then point to no one, even for someone holding the database and the account list: only numbers remain, which are no longer personal data.

Reporting can be turned off in the machine settings. An installation that never opened an account may also send these measurements: it is then linked to no person, only to a local secret that prevents one actor from counting a thousand times.

Approximate location

To place machines on an operations map, we may derive a city from the IP address when a machine registers. The address is resolved and DISCARDED in the same step: it is written neither to the database nor to a log. Resolution uses a local file, without querying a third-party service that would otherwise receive every player’s address.

Coordinates are rounded to a tenth of a degree, about ten kilometres: a city is located, not a home. Places with fewer than three machines are not shown, since a town with only one would point to its owner. This location stays approximate: a machine behind a VPN appears where it exits, and that is its right.

Purchases

Direct sales of software and licences go through PayPal, which processes the payment: we do not see your card data. We keep the order, invoice and licence for ten years, as required by accounting obligations. They appear in “My purchases”.

A purchase made on a partner site from a game page is completed on that site, under its own privacy policy: we receive no data from it.

Newsletter

The launch list sign-up form is no longer offered. Addresses registered earlier, with their language and proof of the choice made, are only used to send Nelfe Play news to people who confirmed it. The legal basis is consent, which you can withdraw at any time. They are kept until unsubscription, and at most three years after the last interaction; minimal data may be kept to honour an unsubscription.

Requests from creators, rights holders and professionals

We process the identity and contact details provided, the organisation, the public link, project information, the message and, where applicable, the declaration of authority in order to review and answer the request.

The legal basis is pre-contractual steps requested by the person and our legitimate interest in handling genuine professional requests. Requests with no follow-up or inactive are deleted at the latest twelve months after the last exchange, unless needed for an agreement or dispute.

Rights holder claims

A person claiming rights over a title sends us their organisation’s name and the documents they consider useful. We keep them while reviewing the request and, if it succeeds, for the duration of the relationship. No automatic rule grants these rights: a person reviews and decides.

A title’s audience figures are only open to its verified rights holder. They are not public.

Game identification index

We keep an index of game file fingerprints, used to recognise a title without relying on its name. This index contains no personal data: only file fingerprints and game names, independent of any person.

Technical security data

Our host may log the IP address, time, user agent and requested resource. Nelfe Play uses a temporary, secretly signed fingerprint of the connection address to limit abuse; the raw address is not stored in the forms database. This relies on our legitimate interest in securing the service. Security logs are kept for at most six months.

Recipients and processors

Our processors are the host Scaleway (France), Gandi for sending emails (France), YouTube (Google Ireland Limited) for published videos, PayPal for payments and browser push services. Public pages and replays are copied as backups to GitHub and GitLab, in the United States: these copies only contain information that is already public. The NelfeTech platform receives your player identity when you link your account to it, never your email address.

We sell no personal data. The site loads no ad network, social tracker or third-party analytics tool.

Your rights

Depending on the processing, you may request access, rectification, erasure, restriction or portability of your data, or object to the processing. Where it relies on consent, you can withdraw it at any time without affecting earlier operations.

Write to contact@nelfeplay.com stating your request. Proportionate proof of identity may be requested if necessary. You may lodge a complaint with the CNIL (cnil.fr).

Security and changes

We use encrypted transport, access controls, prepared queries, form protections and data minimisation. No internet service can promise absolute security.

Any significant change to this policy is dated and explained.

Challenges

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
