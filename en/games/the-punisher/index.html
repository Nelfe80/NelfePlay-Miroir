The story of the game

The why and the how

The Punisher is a Capcom beat 'em up released in arcades in Japan in May 1993, then in North America in July. It features Marvel Comics hero Frank Castle, with Nick Fury, S.H.I.E.L.D. agent, as the second player, setting out to kill the Kingpin and dismantle his organization. The game inaugurates the partnership between Capcom and Marvel, from which the Marvel vs. Capcom series would be born. It was a big hit in arcades and remains regarded as one of the best comic adaptations.

How it was made

The game runs on the CP System Dash board, an improved version of the CP System capable of displaying more than ten enemies on screen without slowdown. Noritaka Funamizu directs it, Yoshiki Okamoto produces it, and Akira Yasuda, Jun Keiba and Junichi Ohno are credited with the design. The music is by Yoko Shimomura and Isao Abe. A pre-release version contained removed elements, such as rocket launchers. A CPS Changer version was announced but never released.

What makes it unique

In the vein of Final Fight and Captain Commando, you advance by eliminating henchmen and then the bosses of each level. The two heroes are almost identical in abilities. Many weapons can be picked up: bats, Japanese swords, knives, shuriken, lead pipes, tires or a homemade flamethrower. Their durability is shown next to the health and they eventually break. The story begins with the death of Castle's family in Central Park, and leads from the illegal casino to the Kingpin's tower.

Good to know

Weapons drop from enemies or come out of broken containers, like treasure that gives bonus points when picked up, jewels included. The real difficulty is keeping an effective weapon to the end without seeing it break, while saving your health for the final showdown against the Kingpin. The weapon's durability is shown next to the health: you can thus tell how many hits remain before it breaks.

Its influence

A Sega Genesis version, developed by Sculptured Software, came out in February 1995 in North America with reduced graphics, sound and enemies, and was a commercial failure. In September 2024, the game joined the Marvel vs. Capcom Fighting Collection: Arcade Classics, the first time the original arcade game was ported unmodified to consoles. The game is seen as one of the best beat 'em ups of all time.

Challenges

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
