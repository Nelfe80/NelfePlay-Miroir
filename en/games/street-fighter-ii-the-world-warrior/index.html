The story of the game

The why and the how

Street Fighter II: The World Warrior is a Capcom fighting game released in arcades in 1991, the fourteenth game on the CP System board. Designed by Yoshiki Okamoto and Akira Yasuda, already the authors of Final Fight, it extends the 1987 Street Fighter, whose six-button version had sparked interest. About 25 million people had played it in the United States by 1994, with more than 200,000 cabinets and 15 million games sold, all versions combined, worldwide.

How it was made

Development lasted about two years with 35 to 40 people, with Noritaka Funamizu producing, Akira Nishitani on game design and Akira Yasuda on character design. The combo system came about by accident: during a test of the car bonus stage, the team noticed that a second hit could be added, and decided to leave the feature hidden. Yoko Shimomura wrote most of the music, the only game in the series she worked on. Nishitani had also imagined a two-screen cabinet and a ten-player battle.

What makes it unique

Eight fighters are playable: Ryu and Ken, from the first game, and six international newcomers. Each has their own style and special moves. An eight-way joystick and six strength buttons, three punches and three kicks, strike a balance between power and speed. A second player can join at any time for a head-to-head duel. The solo mode takes the player to the four Grand Masters, opponents controlled only by the computer, including Sagat, after the seven other fighters.

Good to know

To win a round, you must empty the opponent's life gauge or have more of it when time runs out. The first to win two rounds wins. After every third solo fight, a bonus stage lets you earn points by smashing a car, wooden barrels or metal drums. The world map automatically selects the location of the next fight.

Its influence

Street Fighter II became a pop-culture phenomenon and is often cited among the greatest games of all time. It popularized the fighting genre in the 1990s, kept arcades going and inspired amateur tournaments. Its many revisions, Champion Edition in March 1992, Hyper Fighting in December 1992, Super Street Fighter II in September 1993, extended its life, with more than 6.3 million SNES cartridges sold.

Challenges

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
