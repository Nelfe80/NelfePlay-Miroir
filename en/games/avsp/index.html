The story of the game

The why and the how

Alien vs. Predator is a 1994 beat 'em up video game developed and released by Capcom for the CPS-2 arcade game system, based on the science fiction franchise of the same name. It introduces an original fighter, Lt. Kurosawa. It is set in San Drad, California, overrun by the Xenomorphs: the cyborgs Major Dutch Schaefer and Lieutenant Linn Kurosawa, abandoned by their superiors, are saved by a pair of Predators who offer an alliance. The game was very well received by the public and media publications.

What makes it unique

The game uses an eight-directional joystick and three buttons, to attack, jump and shoot. The default cabinet allows as many as three players simultaneously. Four characters are available: the cyborg soldiers Dutch and Linn, and two Predators, a Hunter and a Warrior. Linn wields a katana, the Hunter a bladed naginata staff and the Warrior an extendable spear, while Dutch hits with his cybernetic arm and can hold and swing any melee weapon dropped by another character. Seven stages lead to the aliens' hive and the plot by Weyland-Yutani.

Good to know

Each character is equipped with a projectile weapon: a rapid-firing handgun for Linn, a smart gun in Dutch's cybernetic arm, and shoulder-mounted energy weapons for the Predators. Ammunition is represented by a meter near the bottom of the screen; when it is depleted, the character is unable to fire until it refills. Linn's refills the fastest and allows the most shots, but she is completely defenseless while she reloads. Weapons such as grenade launchers and flamethrowers can be found or taken from fallen enemies, with limited ammunition. Jewels give bonus points, and food and medicine restore health.

Its influence

Despite being very well received by the public and media publications, the game has only one home system release, included in the Capcom Home Arcade in 2019. The plot reveals that the Alien presence on Earth is the result of a bio-war project headed by the renegade General Bush working for the Weyland-Yutani corporation.

Challenges

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
