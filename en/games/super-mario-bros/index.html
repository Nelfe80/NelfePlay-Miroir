The story of the game

The why and the how

Super Mario Bros. was released in September 1985 in Japan for the Famicom, the Japanese version of the NES. Directed and produced by Shigeru Miyamoto, it is the successor to the 1983 arcade game Mario Bros. and the first game in the Super Mario series. Mario, or his brother Luigi in the multiplayer mode, traverses the Mushroom Kingdom to rescue Princess Toadstool from King Koopa, later named Bowser. It came out in North America in 1986, in PAL regions in 1987, and in arcades on the Nintendo VS. System in early 1986.

How it was made

Shigeru Miyamoto and Takashi Tezuka designed the game as a culmination of the team's experience on Devil World, Excitebike and Kung Fu. Miyamoto wanted a more colorful platform game with a scrolling screen and larger characters. The first level, World 1-1, was designed as a tutorial for the basics of the genre. In December 1984, the team created a prototype in which a 16x32-pixel rectangle moved around a single screen. The sky-based bonus stages are a remnant of an unused rocket ship idea. Koji Kondo's soundtrack is one of the earliest in video games.

What makes it unique

Super Mario Bros. is a side-scrolling platform game: you move right to the flagpole that ends each level. Bricks marked with a question mark hide coins or an item. The Super Mushroom doubles Mario's size and lets him break bricks. The Fire Flower gives him fireballs and the Starman gives him temporary invincibility. You mostly defeat enemies by jumping on them, and Koopa shells can be used as projectiles. Eight worlds of four levels each end in Bowser's castle.

Good to know

Mario loses a life if he takes damage while small, falls off the screen or runs out of time. The Super Mushroom protects him from a single hit, but a fall always kills him. To earn lives, you look for green 1-Up Mushrooms hidden in bricks, collect 100 coins, defeat several enemies in a row with a Koopa shell or bounce on enemies successively without touching the ground. Warp pipes let you skip directly to later worlds, which changes how a run is managed.

Its influence

Super Mario Bros. is frequently cited as one of the greatest and most influential video games of all time, particularly admired for its precise controls. It is one of the best-selling games, with more than 58 million copies sold worldwide. Alongside the NES, it is credited as a key factor in reviving the video game industry after the 1983 crash, and helped popularize the side-scrolling platform genre. Its soundtrack is often named among the best in video games. It began a franchise including a long-running game series, an animated TV series and several films.

Challenges

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
