The story of the game

The why and the how

Rygar is a platform game developed and published by Tecmo for arcades in 1986. The player takes the role of a legendary warrior who must defeat Ligar, a monster with the body of a man and the head of a lion, presented as the world's new dominator. The story fits in an opening text: 4.5 billion years have passed since the Earth's creation. The designer is Hideo Yoshizawa. The game was ported to the NES in 1987, then to many computers and consoles.

How it was made

In the original Japanese version, the hero is unnamed and called only the Legendary Warrior. The final boss is written in katakana as ライガー, which can be romanized as Rygar or Ligar, since Japanese does not distinguish L from R. When the game was translated, the villain kept the name Ligar and the hero was mistakenly called Rygar. Tecmo also wanted to force players to develop the necessary skills: the Buy-In, which lets you continue by paying, is unavailable in levels 21 to 27.

What makes it unique

Rygar is a side-scrolling platformer. Its hero jumps, ducks, attacks and climbs ropes with a single weapon, the Diskarmor, a razor-sharp spinning shield thrown at a distance like a yo-yo. There is no health bar: contact with an enemy or a projectile, or a fall, kills him at once. Five temporary power-ups are found in stones: Sun, Crown, Cross, Tiger and Star. Jumping on an enemy's head also stuns it.

Good to know

Rygar is mostly about managing resources. The hero starts with three lives, and each level has a time limit: past it, a powerful fire monster appears and chases him, faster the longer he is evaded. Five types of items can be collected: bonus points, power-ups, extra lives, additional time and screen bombs. Power-ups are lost on death. At the end of each of the 27 levels, a safe zone tallies the score. The Buy-In lets you continue from your furthest progression, but only up to level 20.

Its influence

The 1987 NES version, released in Japan as Argos no Senshi: Hachamecha Daishingeki, added action RPG elements with a semi-open-ended exploration in which the hero grows stronger. It was the most lauded version and is now recognised as an important early example of the metroidvania sub-genre. The game got a remake on PlayStation 2 in 2002, Rygar: The Legendary Adventure, then a remaster on Wii in 2008. As of June 2007, the various versions of Rygar had sold 1.5 million units.

  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
