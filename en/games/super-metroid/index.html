The story of the game

The why and the how

Super Metroid came out on the Super Nintendo in 1994, almost ten years after the first Metroid. It is the third game in the series, after Metroid II on Game Boy. Bounty hunter Samus Aran chases Ridley, leader of the Space Pirates, who has stolen a baby Metroid from the Ceres colony. The hunt takes her to the planet Zebes, a vast world whose areas are linked by doors and elevators. The team had waited for the moment when a true action game would be needed.

How it was made

Nintendo R&D1 assembled a staff of fifteen, managed by Gunpei Yokoi. Yoshio Sakamoto wrote and directed, Makoto Kano produced, and Intelligent Systems handled the programming. Nintendo took half a year to approve the project, then development lasted two years. The goal was a good action game, in which Samus can fire in every direction while moving. The large map raised a problem of organizing the graphic data, which the team solved by splitting it into smaller parts. Kenji Yamamoto composed the main theme by humming it on his motorcycle.

What makes it unique

Samus explores Zebes looking for upgrades that open up areas that were out of reach: the Morph Ball, the Speed Booster, the Hi-Jump Boots, the Grapple Beam, the X-ray Scope. An automap and an inventory screen guide progress, and save stations break the adventure into manageable pieces. Wall jumping and the Moon Walk widen her moves. At the end she receives the Hyper Beam, a weapon born from the energy of the Super Metroid. The reserve tanks drain by themselves when her energy hits zero.

Good to know

Here, what is measured is time. Super Metroid has three endings depending on how long the run takes: Samus appears with or without her suit. The best ending comes from finishing the game in under three hours. Rescuing the Dachora and the Etecoons, friendly creatures met along the way, also shows them leaving the planet in the distance. To save seconds, speedrunners use wall jumps or bomb jumps to reach some areas earlier than planned, which the developers partly intended.

Its influence

Super Metroid was acclaimed for its atmosphere, gameplay, music and graphics, and it is often cited among the greatest games of all time. It shipped 1.42 million copies worldwide by late 2003. Alongside Castlevania: Symphony of the Night, it is credited with establishing the metroidvania genre, which inspired numerous indie games. Metroid Fusion and Metroid Prime followed in 2002, and arrangements of its themes were used in Metroid Prime.

Challenges

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
