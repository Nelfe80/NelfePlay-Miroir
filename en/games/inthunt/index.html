The story of the game

The why and the how

In the Hunt is a 1993 scrolling shooter developed and published by Irem for arcades, running on the Irem M-92 hardware. According to Arcade History, it was released in the USA in April 1993. The player assumes control of the Granvia, a submarine tasked with overthrowing the Dark Anarchy Society, or D.A.S., before they activate their doomsday device over a post-apocalyptic Earth. Its gameplay is described as a cross between Metal Slug and R-Type, even though Metal Slug would only come out in 1996. It met with mixed reception, specifically for the home conversions.

How it was made

The game was designed by a group of Irem employees who would later found Nazca Corporation, the creators of Metal Slug. Kazuma Kujo served as designer, writing the concept and overseeing development. Irem's management had already settled on a shoot 'em up, but Kujo decided to make it for co-op play, disliked genre conventions such as forced scrolling and wanted a theme different from the standard space motive. The idea of playing as submarines was inspired during Kujo's leisure near a fountain, from the sound of the water. Artist Akio Oyabu served as graphic designer.

What makes it unique

In the Hunt is an underwater-themed scrolling shooter. The game does not scroll automatically, as the player is able to move the Granvia at their own will. In each level, the objective is to destroy enemies, collect power-up items and avoid projectiles. Simultaneous multiplayer is a main focus. According to the press, strong points are the huge number of enemies on screen for the bulk of the game and the minimal slowdown on PlayStation, though common complaints concern the lack of innovation.

Its influence

In Japan, Game Machine listed In the Hunt as the sixth most popular arcade game of May 1993, and RePlay reported it as the fifth most popular. The game was ported to the Sega Saturn and PlayStation in 1995 by Tsuji Jimusho and SIMS, followed by a Windows version by Xing Entertainment in 1997, released internationally by THQ under their Kokopeli Digital Studios label. A Super Nintendo conversion shown playable at the 1994 Consumer Electronics Show was never released. In 2019, Hamster Corporation released it in their Arcade Archives series for Switch and PlayStation 4.

Challenges

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): a single life, from start to finish.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): a single credit each, several players in the same run.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): each player's score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): the 1CC of each game and its special modes, added together.
  • SPEEDRUN: each trial (a level, a boss, the whole game) has its own ranking by time.
  • CHALLENGE: a challenge with set rules (one weapon, one constraint), from start to finish, each with its own ranking by time. Challenges are not certified.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
