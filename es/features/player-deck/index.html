What is a Player Deck?

A Player Deck is an interface drawn for one specific game, shown around its picture while you play. Its plates show live the score of each stage, stocks, timers, values the game keeps to itself, such as Battle Garegga's rank or TGM2's grade, and your provisional place on the Nelfe Play leaderboard.

The game stays in the middle. The Deck uses the space the screen leaves free on the sides.

The story

What the game hides

Many games keep counts they never display. Battle Garegga sets its difficulty on a hidden rank: its Deck shows it as a percentage and as a curve over the whole game, 100% being the ceiling of the Normal setting. TGM2 computes an internal grade from 9 to S9: its Deck shows it with its decay over time and what the Grand Master title requires. Other Decks follow the score of each stage, lives, bombs, Sonic's rings or Metal Slug 3's freed prisoners.

When it opens

The Deck opens with the games launched from the Nelfe Play buttons: CHALLENGE in the World Scoring panel, LAUNCH THE SPEEDRUN and LAUNCH THE CHALLENGE in the Challenges & Speedruns panel. It is on by default. A short Select + A switches to the next Deck or turns it off, and the choice is kept for each game and each mode. A game launched without these buttons, a Multiplayer game or a Mass Run is played without a Deck.

Where it shows

Nelfe Play prepares a RetroBat decoration for the screen: a background with a slot kept for the game, drawn by RetroArch. The Deck plates sit on that background, left and right of the picture, or on one side depending on the game. Game and Deck are composed within a 16:9 frame at most: on a wider screen, the whole stays centred between two black bars. On some screens, the game is slightly reduced to leave the Deck its minimum space.

Where the values come from

Nelfe Play's API follows the memory addresses declared in the game's .MEM file and passes the values to the Deck on the player's machine. A second file, the .SMEM, says what each value means: name, unit, scale. Nothing is written into the game and the ROM stays untouched. When a value is missing, the Deck shows NO DATA or two dashes.

The live rank

At launch, Nelfe Play reads the leaderboard of the game and mode being played. During the game, the rank is recomputed on the machine at every new score, with no network call. This rank stays provisional: it ignores scores sent by other players during your game, and only the certified score enters the leaderboard. In a timed trial, the plate shows the target rank and the time to beat.

Before, after and between games

For World Scoring games and trials launched from their panels, the start and recap screens are Nelfe Play's, and the Deck hands them its recap lines, for example the highest rank reached in Battle Garegga or the best grade in TGM2. A new game in the same launch resets the counters, a continue keeps them. Outside a game, the Deck stays hidden.

Drawn for each game

Each Deck has its theme: its plates, its layout, colours measured on the game's own screens. The fonts are free fonts (Press Start 2P, Silkscreen) and no picture, sprite or logo comes from the game. The themes are published under the CC BY 4.0 licence.

A missing value is shown as missing: NO DATA or two dashes.

FAQ

Does the Player Deck modify the game?

No. The ROM stays untouched and nothing is written into the game. The values come from Nelfe Play's API, which follows the addresses declared in the game's .MEM file.

When does it open?

With the games launched from the Nelfe Play buttons: CHALLENGE (World Scoring), LAUNCH THE SPEEDRUN and LAUNCH THE CHALLENGE (Challenges & Speedruns).

Can it be turned off?

Yes. It is on by default; a short Select + A switches to the next Deck or to Off, and the choice is kept per game and per mode. The decoration already in place stays until the next launch.

Does it cover the game picture?

No. Its plates sit around the picture. Only the start and recap screens fill the screen, for a few seconds.

What about an ultra-wide screen?

Game and Deck keep a 16:9 frame at most, centred, with black bars on each side.

Is the rank shown during the game official?

No. It is computed on the leaderboard read at launch and stays provisional. Only the certified score enters the leaderboard.

Which games have one?

25 games so far (Arcade, Mega Drive, NES, Super Nintendo). The up-to-date list is on the Games page, with the Player Deck filter.

Is it available in Multiplayer or Mass Run?

No. These modes keep their own interface.

What do I need to use it?

A Windows PC with RetroBat and Nelfe Play installed. The Deck ships with Nelfe Play and follows games launched under RetroArch.

Can players make their own Deck?

Not today. The Decks in service are the Nelfe Play ones.

Los desafíos

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): un solo crédito, en uno de los modos del juego. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): el juego terminado con ese crédito.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): una sola vida, de principio a fin.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): un solo crédito cada uno, varios jugadores en la misma partida.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): la puntuación de cada uno hasta su primera vida perdida, varios jugadores en la misma partida.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): el 1CC de cada juego y sus modos especiales, sumados.
  • SPEEDRUN: cada prueba (un nivel, un jefe, el juego entero) tiene su propia clasificación por tiempo.
  • CHALLENGE: un desafío con reglas impuestas (un arma, una restricción), de principio a fin, cada uno con su propia clasificación por tiempo. Los desafíos no están certificados.
  • Mass Run: carreras en directo, decenas de jugadores en el mismo nivel desde la misma salida. Cada juego tiene su rating por formato; los diez primeros de la clasificación permanente Mass Speedrun de cada juego suman puntos. Las carreras no están certificadas.
