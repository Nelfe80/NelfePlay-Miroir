Leaderboards

Leaderboards explained

Every performance has its own leaderboard. The players leaderboard brings them together through the place each player holds in them: a 19XX score is never compared to a Sonic time.

Three worlds that never mix

A score is ranked in its world: at home, in a venue, or in a contest. The three do not carry the same guarantees. In a venue, the measurement happens on a cabinet the player does not control; at home, on their own machine.

Every leaderboard, and every record, is read world by world. The “all” view is for browsing: it shows the world of each line.

The World Scoring challenges

A game open to World Scoring is ranked by score, in several challenges. Each one is an official leaderboard of its own.

1CC

One-Credit Challenge: a single credit, in one of the modes of the game. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): the game finished on that credit.

1LC

One-Life Challenge: the score reached with a single life, from start to finish.

1CC MULTI

A single credit each, several players in the same run.

1LC MULTI

Each player’s score up to their first life lost, several players in the same run.

1CC MM

Multi Modes: the 1CC of a game and of its special modes, added together.

Speedruns and challenges

A trial is ranked by time. A speedrun can be a level, a boss or the whole game; a challenge sets its own rules, such as finishing a mission with the knife.

The official time is the cabinet’s, counted frame by frame, from the moment the game hands control to the player until the finish event. The time shown by the game is displayed next to it.

  • Everyone starts the same way: the game’s own start, or an official start state, checked by its fingerprint.
  • During an attempt: no pause, no save state, no rewind, no fast forward, no cheats.
  • A challenge can hold a game value for the whole attempt (a set weapon, for example). The cabinet proves it held it on every frame, otherwise the time is not sent.
  • Each player’s best time counts. On a tie, the first to arrive stays ahead.
  • A speedrun is certified like a score: a game that slows down, even slightly, voids the attempt, because time is counted in frames.
  • A challenge is not certified: it changes the game (invincibility, set weapons) and may have its own measurement definition. Its time is checked (cabinet signature, consistency), without claiming a certified record.
  • When a trial’s rules change, a new version opens with a fresh leaderboard: the old version is retired.

The Kinch score

To bring a game’s levels together without adding times that do not compare, NelfePlay uses the Kinch score from speedcubing. On each trial, the score is 100 × the record ÷ your best time: the record is worth 100, and the closer you get, the more you score.

A game’s score is the average of your scores over all its trials of the same type. A trial you have not played counts as 0: the score rewards those who play the whole game.

Full game categories

Any% (finishing the game as fast as possible, by any allowed means) is ranked on its own, by its time. So will be the other full game categories, such as 100% or Low%.

The players leaderboard

With no game chosen, the page shows the players leaderboard. Each leaderboard gives points to its top ten: 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2 and 1. A player adds up their points across every leaderboard of their world.

  • A World Scoring leaderboard: one game and one challenge (the 1CC of 19XX, the 1LC of Sonic).
  • A game’s levels, ranked by the Kinch score: one single leaderboard for all the Sonic levels.
  • Each full game category (Sonic Any%), ranked by its time.
  • A game’s challenges, ranked by the Kinch score.
  • On equal points: the most first places, then the most leaderboards, then the first to arrive.
  • The Challenge filter narrows the players leaderboard to one type: 1CC, 1LC, SPEEDRUN, CHALLENGE, Mass Run…

Mass Run

A Mass Run race brings dozens of players together on the same level, from the same start, each on their own cabinet. It gives no points per race: each player has a rating per game and per format (Mass Speedrun, Mass Survival), computed with OpenSkill, the engine behind the MULTIPLAYER group rankings.

Only the finishing order within a race counts: the level drawn plays no part in the calculation.

  • Mass Speedrun: finishers in order of their times, then everyone who did not finish, tied for last place.
  • Mass Survival: the survivors tied at the top, then the eliminated players, from the latest to the earliest.
  • A race counts from two human players, with one single place per account and per cabinet. Ghosts, bots and runners the race server flags as suspect never enter it.
  • The rating shown is a cautious one: 1000 + 48 × (mu − 3σ) (a new runner starts at 1000). σ, the uncertainty, drops with each race and rises again when you do not race: the rating shown then slowly drops.
  • The permanent leaderboard requires 5 races; before that, the rating is provisional.
  • On the overall leaderboard, under the Mass Run filter, the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2 and 1 points (home world).
  • A Mass Run race is not certified: it has no replay. Its result is signed by the race server, and each runner by their cabinet.

The sum of levels

When a player has a time on every level of a game, the game’s Kinch leaderboard shows the sum of their times, next to the sum of the records and their Any%. It is for information: each level stays an independent performance, and this sum enters no leaderboard.

A certified score

Every score and every time leaves the cabinet with a signed passport: the emulator, the game’s measurement definition, the settings and the run itself. The site checks it before publishing it, and the score log is sealed on the Blockchain.

  • Published: the score enters the leaderboard.
  • Pending: the emulator or its settings are not certified yet. The score is kept and published as soon as they are.
  • Refused: the run breaks the leaderboard’s rules (a forbidden setting, a loaded save, a different definition).

Los desafíos

  • 1CC (One-Credit Challenge): un solo crédito, en uno de los modos del juego. ✓1CC (One-Credit Clear): el juego terminado con ese crédito.
  • 1LC (One-Life Challenge): una sola vida, de principio a fin.
  • 1CC MULTI (One-Credit Challenge, multiplayer): un solo crédito cada uno, varios jugadores en la misma partida.
  • 1LC MULTI (One-Life Challenge, multiplayer): la puntuación de cada uno hasta su primera vida perdida, varios jugadores en la misma partida.
  • 1CC MM (One-Credit Challenge, Multi Modes): el 1CC de cada juego y sus modos especiales, sumados.
  • SPEEDRUN: cada prueba (un nivel, un jefe, el juego entero) tiene su propia clasificación por tiempo.
  • CHALLENGE: un desafío con reglas impuestas (un arma, una restricción), de principio a fin, cada uno con su propia clasificación por tiempo. Los desafíos no están certificados.
  • Mass Run: live races, dozens of players on the same level from the same start. Each game has its rating per format; the top ten of each game’s permanent Mass Speedrun leaderboard score points. Races are not certified.
